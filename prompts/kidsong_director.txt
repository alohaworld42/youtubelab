- The very first shot of the whole video (verse 1, first shot) MUST be a wide establishing shot

- Within a single verse, vary the shot_type — never use the exact same shot_type three times in

- Use camera moves (a push-in, a pull-back, a gentle pan) sparingly: at most 2 non-static camera

- If a verse's lines are an EXACT repeat of an earlier verse (a chorus / refrain), do not invent

  chorus needs more shots than the original verse had, cycle back through the original's shots.
- Every "action" must describe one concrete, visible thing happening on screen — the story
  subject's event, a child's action, or both — taken directly from THAT SHOT's own lyric_span,
  not a vague mood, not a summary of the whole verse.
- "setting" is a PLACE, never an event. Describe only the location and its props ("a bright
  cheerful bathroom with a white pedestal sink, a round mirror and colorful toiletry bottles").
  NEVER put people in "setting": no character names, no "the three friends", no "together", no
  verbs about what anyone is doing. "Amira, Kofi and Zuri are rinsing their mouths" is WRONG —
  that belongs in "action", and the setting is just the bathroom. The setting is pasted straight
  into the render prompt after the head count, so people in it contradict the head count and the
  renderer draws duplicate children to satisfy both.
- ONE VERSE, ONE LOCATION. Every shot in a verse must read as the SAME continuous moment: same
  place, same lighting, filmed from different angles and distances. Within that, the verse's
  shots should form a small TEMPORAL ARC that follows the lyric — setup, then the event the
  lyric describes, then a reaction — not a single frozen pose repeated from different cameras.
  Each INDIVIDUAL shot still shows exactly ONE activity happening at once (see the next rule for
  what's forbidden); it is the sequence across the verse's shots that is allowed, and expected,
  to move. Worked example (Hickory Dickory Dock, verse 2, "The clock struck one, the mouse ran
  down / And ran right through the town"): shot A — the clock face strikes one; shot B — the
  mouse scampers down the clock case; shot C — Zuri points after it and giggles. Three shots, one
  activity each, one location, one continuous moment: this is CORRECT.
- FORBIDDEN: two or more children doing two or more DIFFERENT things AT THE SAME TIME in one

  is three separate beats, not one shot — either split them into consecutive shots in the arc
  above, or pick the single activity they can all share in that shot ("the kids blow bubbles and
  watch them float up"). A 2-3 second shot cannot show three unrelated activities at once, so the
  renderer crowds the frame and the shots stop matching each other.
- "setting" must be IDENTICAL, word for word, across all shots within the same verse, matching

- CHANGE THE LOCATION between verses when the lyrics allow it (bathroom → kitchen → backyard) —
